# Council Review - SME Income First, Then Larger Acquisitions
**Counsel from the identified panel | August 2026 | Internal draft - codes per Project V Index - not for distribution**

---

## Synthesis

The instinct is right and the market confirms it: boring, cash-yielding SMEs in Cyprus and Greece trade at roughly 4 to 6x EBITDA while the European mid-market prints 8.6x - so a disciplined buyer earns ~10 to 12% cash-on-cash from day one and holds a 3-turn multiple arbitrage for whenever aggregation or exit suits him. Nobody occupies this ground in Cyprus - there is no Cyprus-domiciled SME buyout or income fund at all - and in Greece the nearest funds (SMERemediumCap, EOS, Elikonos, SouthBridge) are turnaround, growth or mezz players, not income compounders. The council's verdict: proceed, but fix three things the current framing gets wrong - the vehicle (income needs an evergreen or distributing structure, not a standard drawdown fund), the instrument (structured equity with contractual yield, not plain common equity), and the sequencing (this strategy must feed the Project V story, never delay its critical path).

## Implications

- **Regulatory.** The club phase must be structured as genuine co-investment (deal-by-deal SPVs, investors deciding per deal) - a pooled blind commitment with a defined policy is an AIF and needs the AIFM licence engaged; post-Project V the book folds into a distributing income sub-fund of R. ELTIF 2.0 is the later retail wrapper. CySEC change-of-control process must not be complicated by new regulated activity mid-review.
- **Tax.** Cyprus holdco stack: 12.5% CIT, NID on new equity injections, no WHT on dividends out of the RAIF to non-residents; Greek opcos distribute to the Cyprus holdco under the Parent-Subsidiary Directive at 0% WHT (5% Greek dividend WHT applies outside it - structure to qualify). Related-party service fees from SVC to portfolio companies priced per Circular 6/2023, CUP method.
- **Liquidity.** Dividends are the liquidity - design distributing share classes from day one (Petri's point) and match any leverage amortisation to payout capacity. No redemption features at the SME book level, ever; if wealth-channel money wants in, it enters through the evergreen sleeve with gates, not through the holdco.
