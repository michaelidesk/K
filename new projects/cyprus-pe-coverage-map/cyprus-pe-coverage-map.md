**Internal - Investment Committee. Prepared 29 August 2026.**
**Coverage question: does this firm allocate coverage time to Cyprus, and under what regional seat?**

Evidence labels used throughout:

- **[REPORTED]** - press or database, source and date named
- **[INFERRED]** - analyst reasoning from labelled inputs, no company invented

## STEP 0 - WHAT REGION IS CYPRUS ACTUALLY IN

The question was tested against three bodies of evidence: fund coverage structures, data-provider classifications, and observed deal behaviour. All three return the same answer.

**Fund coverage structures.** No global fund maintains a Cyprus coverage team, office or country mandate. CVC is the only mega-fund with a dedicated Athens team (>€750m equity deployed in Greece since 2017 per its own releases [REPORTED, CVC 29 Jan 2025]), and its only Cyprus exposure - the Nicosia and Paphos hospitals inside Hellenic Healthcare Group - was executed by a Greek platform company, not by a Cyprus mandate. Blackstone, KKR, BC Partners, Oaktree, Apollo, PIMCO, Apax and Carlyle all show Greek activity run from London or via credit desks; none shows Cyprus coverage [REPORTED, multiple sources per name]. Explicit SEE-mandate funds - Bedminster's Southeast Europe Equity Fund II, Global Finance's South Eastern Europe Fund, Mid Europa, ARX - all exclude Cyprus from their geographies [REPORTED]. The only fund found with Cyprus named inside an SEE mandate is the 2006-vintage, long-defunct 7L Capital Partners Emerging Europe LP ($120.2m, IFC-backed) [FILED, IFC disclosure SPI/24464]. The inversion is telling: CEE Equity Partners, the China-CEE fund, is Nicosia-domiciled and invests everywhere except Cyprus [REPORTED, Preqin].

**Data-provider classification.** Invest Europe's 2025 activity report groups Southern Europe as Greece, Italy, Portugal, Spain; Cyprus appears in no named regional bucket, and it is excluded from the Invest Europe CEE reports [REPORTED, Invest Europe May 2026 - PDF extract, verify against primary before external use]. PitchBook's methodology files Cyprus under "Southern Europe" alongside Andorra, Gibraltar, Malta, Monaco and San Marino - a residual bucket of micro-jurisdictions, not a coverage region [REPORTED, PitchBook methodology page - verify]. Preqin: NO RELIABLE DATA on a formal bucket.

**Observed deal behaviour.** Every institutional equity event of scale on the island since 2018 arrived via Athens balance sheets or Athens-managed platforms: Eurobank-Hellenic Bank (~€1.2b spent, implied 100% equity ~€2.0b [FILED, Eurobank announcements 11 Feb / 11 Jun 2025; INFERRED on the implied total]), Alpha Bank-AstroBank (€205m [FILED, Alpha PR 3 Nov 2025]), CVC/HHG's hospital bolt-ons. Third-party institutional capital touched Cyprus only as credit - Apollo and PIMCO bought roughly €5b GBV of Helix NPLs - or as development finance with an expiry date: EBRD's Cyprus mandate was temporary (2014-2020, ~€600m invested, final Bank of Cyprus stake sold September 2025 [FILED, EBRD]). The sole live institutional equity mandate on the island is the EIF-run Cyprus Equity Fund seeding 33East's €26m seed-stage vehicle [FILED, EIF; REPORTED, CBN 21 Jan 2025]. The domicile role, by contrast, is quantitative: €11.2b of CySEC-supervised fund AUM at the latest quarter, of which only ~€2.9b (~26%) is invested in the Cypriot economy [FILED via CySEC statistics, Cyprus Mail 8 Jul 2026]; 260+ active management companies and UCIs [REPORTED, CIFA/Cyprus Mail Feb 2026]; historically ~35% of Russian FDI flows routed through Cypriot SPEs [REPORTED, Journal of Money Laundering Control 2019].

**Classification the evidence supports.** Cyprus is a **fund and holding domicile first, and a Greece-adjunct market second**. It is not SE Europe as funds define SE Europe, and it is not a standalone EMEA-tail market. No primary sponsor buyout of a Cypriot operating company appears anywhere in the retrieved record before 2025, and the first and only institutional PE transaction on the island - Blackstone/HIP's Olympic Lagoon Resorts purchase - is a hotel real-estate deal executed by the sponsor's Southern Europe hotel platform, not a country bet [REPORTED, CBN 2025].

**What follows for this memo:** the region mapped in Step 1 is **Greece plus Cyprus**, because that is the coverage unit the market actually uses. The coverage recommendation implied throughout is that Cyprus attaches to the Athens/Greece seat for origination and to fund-formation/domicile services for everything else. It does not earn a seat of its own on current evidence.

### 1.5 Leverage availability

**Greece.** The four systemic banks (Piraeus, Alpha, NBG, Eurobank) are again the primary acquisition lenders; the market is "heavily dependent on local banks," conservative under ECB leveraged-finance guidance, with junior/hybrid debt reserved for large infrastructure and complex M&A [REPORTED, IFLR]. Published LBO margins and leverage multiples: NO RELIABLE DATA. Private credit funds finance sponsors and portfolio companies for LBOs and growth, but private credit to listed corporates remains uncommon [REPORTED, Chambers Private Credit 2026]; named lender league tables could not be verified. Listed common bond loans function as acquisition currency (Ideal Holdings funded 65% of the €100m Attica Dept Stores buyout with a €100m listed bond [FILED]). The enabler is the NPL cleanup: Greek NPL ratio 49.1% at peak (Mar 2017) to 3.6% (Jun 2025), the lowest since euro entry [FILED, Bank of Greece FSR Oct 2025].

**Cyprus.** The banking system holds €17.4b of excess liquidity (end-2025 [FILED via CBC, Cyprus Mail 12 Jun 2026]), deposits exceed loans by a record €30.8b, the loan/deposit ratio sits near 50% and sector LCR at 319% vs an EU median of 192% [REPORTED]. Yet there is **no domestic LBO debt market**: no Cypriot bank has a disclosed sponsor-finance business, and no locally domiciled private credit fund lending on-island could be found - NO RELIABLE DATA of any [INFERRED: post-2013 supervisory scar tissue, a concentrated two-bank system under SSM, and a real-estate-collateral lending culture]. Cyprus NPLs: from a system peak of €27b (2014) to a sector ratio of 3.2% at Dec 2025, below the EU average for the first time since 2014 [FILED, Central Bank of Cyprus 18 Feb 2026]. Where bank credit stops in Cyprus, nothing begins: sponsor debt must be imported from Greek banks or international credit.

### 1.6 RRF and EU structural funds

**Greece ("Greece 2.0"):** ~€36b total - €18.22b grants + €17.73b loans (~16% of 2023 GDP), running to Aug 2026 approvals [FILED, EC country page]. The loan facility runs through the banks: RRF debt covers up to 50% of eligible cost at subsidised rates, sponsor equity of at least 20% is mandatory, banks co-finance the balance; ~€13.3b of the loan arm has been combined with bank lending and private equity, mobilising >€30b of investment [FILED, greece20.gov.gr]. Heaviest absorbers: energy transition and digitalisation; tourism qualifies via green/digital capex. **Crowding read [INFERRED]:** at the asset level the structure crowds equity in - it contractually requires sponsor equity - but the subsidised debt tranche competes directly with mezzanine and private credit in the mid-market, consistent with Greek mezzanine funds pivoting toward sponsor situations. This is analyst inference, not a sourced finding.

**Cyprus:** €1.22b total - €1,005m grants + €200.3m loans [FILED, EC 17 Nov 2023]. Largest allocations: green transition/energy, digital, health. The €200m loan component is too small to function as an acquisition-finance channel; Cyprus's RRF is fiscally material and PE-irrelevant, with one exception - it funded the €27m Cyprus Equity Fund that seeded 33East, the island's first institutional VC vehicle [FILED, EIF; INFERRED on the characterisation].

## STEP 2 - SECTOR BY SECTOR

Convention for the verdicts: **contested** = institutional capital is already competing for the assets; **underserved** = investable assets exist and institutional capital is absent or scarce; **structurally uninvestable** = the barrier is ownership, politics or scale, not price.

### 2.1 Financial services, banks and NPLs

**Regional structure.** The defining process of the window is the state's exit and the banks' return to acquirer status. HFSF sold out of all four Greek systemics by end-2024: Alpha 9% to UniCredit (€293.5m, Nov 2023), NBG 22% (~€1.06-1.1b, 8x covered, Nov 2023) then the final 10% (2024), Piraeus 27% (€1.35b, Mar 2024) [FILED/REPORTED per leg]. Sector RoTE now runs 14-16% with cost/income in the mid-30s vs ~50% for European peers [REPORTED, Goldman Sachs 2026 via press]. The Hercules scheme moved ~€50-55b GBV of NPEs into 17 securitisations, creating a servicing industry (doValue Greece >€35b AuM, Cepal ~€26.4b, Intrum Hellas €20.8b; the four largest servicers earned combined net profits of €122m in 2025 [REPORTED]). Insurance rotated twice: Ethniki NBG → CVC (€505m incl. up to €120m earn-out, 2021-22) → Piraeus (€600m, 2025); Eurolife 80% Fairfax → Eurobank (€813m, ~1.45x P/B, 2025-26) [FILED per deal].

**Economics.** Cleaned balance sheets, oligopoly pricing, mid-teens RoTE, and capital-return capacity restored. The winning thesis was buying bank equity or insurance at distressed-to-book prices ahead of the sovereign re-rating; that trade is done [INFERRED].

**Defining transactions:** the six above, plus Attica Bank/Pancreta recap ("CrediaBank": up to €735m recap, Thrivest to 50%+ [FILED, HCC Decision 855/2024]).

**CYPRUS INSIDE THIS SECTOR.** Cyprus banking consolidated into Greek hands inside 30 months: Eurobank built to 100% of Hellenic Bank (~€750m for the 37.5% block at €4.843/share, ~€1.2b total spent, implied 100% equity ~€2.0b [FILED Eurobank; INFERRED implied value]; the €2.4b figure in circulation is not supported by filings); Hellenic bought CNP Cyprus at €182m, ~1x book [FILED, CNP]; Alpha bought AstroBank for at least €205m, creating the #3 bank [FILED, Alpha]. The NPL arc is complete: Helix I €2.8b GBV to Apollo (2019), Helix 2 ~€916m + further tranches to PIMCO (2020-21), Helix 3 €577m GBV + €121m REO for €385m consideration (2021), Hellenic's Starlight €1.32b NPE to PIMCO's Oxalis at an implied €320m (~41% of GBV - a notably high recovery) with the APS servicer to Themis [FILED/REPORTED per deal]. Sector NPL ratio 3.2% (Dec 2025) [FILED, CBC]. Residual distress sits with Kedipes (state AMC, €2.94b recovered), Themis, doValue Cyprus and Gordian. What remains investable at institutional ticket: Bank of Cyprus equity on ATHEX; **Demetra Holdings**, the CSE-listed holding company sitting on ~€425m gross proceeds from its Hellenic exit [INFERRED from share count x price] - a genuine special situation; and the servicing/REO platforms, which are sponsor-owned and will eventually trade.

**VERDICT: contested - and effectively closed.** The two realistic bank entries were taken by Greek strategics at prices no fund matched. The remaining angle is secondary paper (BOC), one cash-shell special situation (Demetra), and eventual servicer exits.

### 2.2 Healthcare and pharma

**Regional structure.** One consolidator (HHG, built by CVC: 11 hospitals, 23 diagnostic centres, >1.4m patients/yr) defined the sector and delivered the region's flagship sponsor exit: 60% to PureHealth for €800m at a €1.3b equity value, €2.2b headline transaction (completed Oct 2025); CVC retains 35% [FILED, CVC/PureHealth]. Pharma manufacturing re-rated harder: Pharmathen €475m (2015) → €1.6b EV (2021). Distressed clinic assets (Euromedica) went lender-led (Farallon + Piraeus), then to Henry Dunant. Bioiatriki - the leading diagnostics group, 56 centres in Greece and Cyprus - remains founder-held with early-stage sale interest reported [REPORTED, HBI 2026]; it is not CVC-owned. Affidea Greece belongs to GBL's pan-European platform.

**Economics.** Greek private health expenditure forecast at +7.4% CAGR to 2028 [REPORTED, Research & Markets]; hospital EBITDA margins: NO RELIABLE DATA published. The HHG exit is the only market-clearing valuation reference for the sector.

**Defining transactions:** Pharmathen (€1.6b, 2021); HHG/PureHealth (€800m/60%, 2025); Ethniki-adjacent bancassurance flows; Euromedica restructuring (2020); Remedica lender-led takeover (2021, below).

**CYPRUS INSIDE THIS SECTOR.** Two real industries exist. (1) **Hospitals:** GESY, the universal health system live since June 2019, converted private provision into reimbursable volume - the 2026 state health budget is €1.51b with ~€870m flowing into GESY, and 75 private hospitals contract into it [FILED/REPORTED]. HHG bought the premium capacity: Apollonion (Nicosia), Aretaeio (~Dec 2022), 50% of American Medical Center, Evangelismos Paphos (Jul 2025) - all prices undisclosed, NO RELIABLE DATA [REPORTED per asset]. What is left independent is sub-scale single hospitals. (2) **Pharma manufacturing, the island's only genuinely exportable industrial franchise:** **Remedica** (Limassol generics) is owned by L1 Health + Blantyre Capital via the 2021 Ascendis Health debt-for-asset restructuring (€447m senior debt exchanged; Ascendis had paid €260m + €75m deferred in 2016) [FILED/REPORTED] - sponsor-held and exit-bound, the single most actionable institutional asset on the island. **Medochemie** (Pittas family): ~$322m revenue, 15 EU-GMP plants across Cyprus, NL and Vietnam, ~2,000 staff, ~28.9% of Cyprus industrial exports [REPORTED, CBN 2026] - strategic scale, family-owned, no process; a succession conversation, not a pipeline entry.

**VERDICT: contested in hospitals (HHG/PureHealth own it); underserved in pharma manufacturing** - two institutional-scale assets, one sponsor-owned and heading to market, one family-held with no local buyer universe.

### 2.3 TMT, software and telecom

**Regional structure.** Greece produced a real software consolidation: two ATHEX take-privates in 2024 - Epsilon Net (founder + NBG + General Atlantic at €12.00/sh, ~€241m tendered [FILED/REPORTED]) and Entersoft (Olympia/Unity to 99.62%) - followed by the Entersoft-SoftOne combination, a national-champion ERP layer riding mandatory e-invoicing [REPORTED; values undisclosed]. Skroutz delivered the e-commerce arc: CVC entry 2020, Blackstone exit May 2026 at €635m EV on revenue that grew €30m → €130m+ [FILED/REPORTED]. Telecom consolidated to three players (United Group bought Nova/Forthnet 2020 and Wind Hellas ~€1b 2021 [FILED, United Group]). The cautionary tales are priced in the record: Viva Wallet at ~$2b (JPM 48.5% for ~€800m, 2022) followed by valuation litigation; InstaShop bought at $360m (2020), resold at $32m (2025) - a >90% write-down [FILED/REPORTED].

**Economics.** Software: recurring-revenue assets scarce enough to be taken private at premiums; multiples undisclosed - NO RELIABLE DATA. Telecom: mature three-player market.

**Defining transactions:** Skroutz (2020/2026), Wind Hellas (~€1b, 2021), Viva Wallet (~€800m/48.5%, 2022), Epsilon Net + Entersoft take-privates (2024), SoftOne combination (2024-25).

**CYPRUS INSIDE THIS SECTOR.** The paradox sector. The island hosts large revenue pools - Wargaming (founder-held, Nicosia HQ retained; exited Russia/Belarus 2022; sold its 13.41% Hellenic stake to Eurobank for €70m [FILED/REPORTED]), Exness (record $5.1tn monthly trading volume Apr 2024; current revenue NO RELIABLE DATA), XM/Trading Point, TheSoul, BrainRocket (600+ Cyprus staff), MY.GAMES (sold by VK for $642m in 2022 to a private buyer) - and the verified macro shows it: ICT GVA €3.59b in 2024, +11.3% real, the fastest-growing sector, with an ICT services surplus of 7.5% of GDP [FILED via CyStat/CBC]. Almost none of it is buyable: founder- and emigre-held, unlevered, no succession pressure, and provenance/sanctions diligence burdens that most LP bases will not carry [INFERRED]. Telecoms: Cyta is state-owned with dormant privatisation politics; Epic belongs to NJJ/Monaco Telecom; Cablenet is controlled by Malta-listed GO plc (>€100m invested, €75m more planned [REPORTED, CBN]); Primetel private, NO RELIABLE DATA. The publicly investable exception: ASBIS (Limassol HQ, Warsaw-listed IT distribution, ~$3.1-3.4b 2024 revenue guidance [FILED, ASBIS IR]) - and, on the CSE, Logicom (FY2024 revenue €1.091b [FILED via press]).

**VERDICT: structurally uninvestable in the profit pools that matter** (founder-held CIF/gaming/media wealth); the residual institutional wedge is picks-and-shovels services to the cluster and the two listed distributors.

### 2.4 Energy, renewables and utilities

**Regional structure.** Greece is the region's institutional energy market: Masdar-Terna at €3.2b EV, the largest energy deal in ATHEX history (2024-25); Macquarie's 50% of Enel Green Power Hellas at ~€980m implied EV for 566MW; CVC in PPC since 2021, topped up with a €1.2b cornerstone in the 2026 €4.25b capital increase (stake ~17%) funding a ~€24b plan to 24.3GW by 2030 [FILED per deal]. Note the correction: CVC does hold PPC.

**Economics.** Operating RES platforms cleared at roughly €1.7-2.7m EV/MW depending on pipeline credit [INFERRED from the two filed deals]. Utility-scale returns anchored to regulated asset bases and CfD-backed pipelines.

**Defining transactions:** Terna/Masdar (€3.2b EV), Enel Hellas/Macquarie (~€250m equity for 50%), PPC/CVC (€1.2b, 2026), HEDNO 49%/Macquarie (€2.1b, 2021).

**CYPRUS INSIDE THIS SECTOR.** An energy system defined by three structural failures and one genuine thesis. EAC remains a state-owned vertically integrated monopoly; the competitive market only went live 1 Oct 2025; household tariffs are among the EU's highest [REPORTED]. The Great Sea Interconnector (~€1.9b budget, €657m EC-committed, cost split CY ~€786m / GR ~€460m) is stalled by Turkish maritime interference and cost disputes [REPORTED, Euronews 19 Aug 2026]. The Vasilikos LNG terminal EPC was terminated in July 2024 with ~€200m of arbitration claims and the EU seeking grant clawback [REPORTED, Cyprus Mail]. Upstream gas (Chevron's Aphrodite, ~$4b development plan approved Feb 2025, first gas ~2032; Eni, ExxonMobil) is IOC territory, not PE. The thesis that is real: ~908MW of installed PV against **28-29% RES curtailment** for lack of storage and interconnection [REPORTED, Balkan Green Energy News] - BESS co-location and aggregation of 5-50MW parks ahead of GSI energisation. No Cypriot RES platform above €100m EV could be verified (Bioland is the named family-held platform; "P.N. Solar" does not verifiably exist - NO RELIABLE DATA). Fuel retail: Petrolina, CSE-listed, family-controlled, FY2025 PBT €10.9m - small-cap.

**VERDICT: underserved in distributed RES/storage, but below institutional ticket size today; structurally uninvestable in utilities and networks.** The sector becomes investable the day GSI has a credible energisation date - that is the trigger to re-underwrite.

### 2.5 Hospitality and tourism

**Regional structure.** The most institutionalised real-economy sector in the region. Sani/Ikos: Oaktree/GSAM build, GIC control at €2.3b (2022) with a €900m capex plan. Blackstone/HIP built a 10-hotel, 2,639-room Greek portfolio (Louis portfolio €178.6m EV 2019; Grand Hyatt Athens €235m 2024; €750m capex programme) [FILED/REPORTED]. H.I.G. consolidated Ella Hotels into a ~€1b Southern Europe platform and took control of the 1,522-room Kipriotis portfolio on Kos (2025). Fairfax took Grivalia Hospitality to 78.4% (implying ~€425m equity value for 100% [INFERRED]). SMERemediumCap runs the distressed-hotel channel (Project Tethys, €285m GBV of hotel NPLs). Greek hotel transaction volume hit €1.119b in 2025, fifth in Europe [REPORTED, HVS].

**Economics.** Fragmented family ownership, brand under-penetration, ADR convergence toward Spain/Italy, and capex-arbitrage repositioning are the return drivers [INFERRED from the platform filings].

**Defining transactions:** Sani/Ikos €2.3b (2022); HIP Greek buildout 2019-2025; Ella/Kipriotis (2024-25); Grivalia/Fairfax (2022); Attica Group €443.8m debt-for-equity (2023, ferries).

**CYPRUS INSIDE THIS SECTOR.** The island's most credible institutional sector, and the only one with a proven sponsor entry. Demand is verified: arrivals passed 4m in 2024 for the first time, revenue €3.2b (+7.3%), Jul 2025 the all-time monthly record [REPORTED, CyStat via press]. Supply is family-held: Tsokkos (30 hotels, 8,000+ rooms), Atlantica (TUI JV, biggest owned chain), Kanika, Constantinou Bros, Louis plc (25 hotels, 13,000+ beds, FY25 revenue €138.6m - CSE take-private in motion via the Dec 2025 scheme [REPORTED, Cyprus Mail]). The precedent transaction: **Blackstone/HIP bought Olympic Lagoon Resorts Paphos (276 rooms) from Kanika/Amathus in 2025, with Kanika retaining management - the first institutional PE hotel deal on the island - and has said publicly it is exploring more** [REPORTED, CBN; counsel confirmation, Scordis Papapetrou]. Melco's >€600m City of Dreams Mediterranean (opened Jul 2023) is a strategic hold, not a PE asset. The Louis restructuring perimeter is the obvious next institutional conversation.

**VERDICT: underserved - the one Cyprus sector where the institutional model is proven and repeatable.** The entry is asset-backed OpCo/PropCo purchases from families that keep the management contract; the constraint is that HIP now has first-mover advantage and an information edge.

### 2.6 Consumer, food and beverage, retail

**Regional structure.** Greek F&B is a dismemberment-and-roll-up trade. CVC bought Vivartia at €600m EV (2020-21) and realised it brand-by-brand: Dodoni to Hellenic Dairies (~€205m, 2025), Barba Stathis to Ideal Holdings (€130m, 9.3x LTM EBITDA, 2025) [FILED, Ideal]. Chipita went to Mondelez at ~€1.7b (~20x). Mid Europa took FAMAR (pharma CDMO, 1,900 staff) from ECM/Metric (2024). Ideal Holdings is the listed roll-up reference, now Oak Hill-backed. The buyer universe for €100-250m EV branded assets is deep: strategics (Mondelez, Hellenic Dairies), listed platforms (Ideal), sponsors.

**Economics.** Branded market leaders clear at high single digits EBITDA (9.3x filed); exits are trade sales, not IPOs [INFERRED from the record].

**Defining transactions:** Chipita (~€1.7b, 2021-22); Vivartia (€600m EV, 2020-21); Dodoni exit (~€205m, 2025); Barba Stathis (€130m/9.3x, 2025); FAMAR (2024, undisclosed).

**CYPRUS INSIDE THIS SECTOR.** Grocery consolidated by a Greek strategic, not PE: Sklavenitis acquired Papantoniou (completed Nov 2024 - note, not 2022), creating a 27-store, 2,350-employee network [REPORTED, Cyprus Mail]. Alphamega remains the only family grocery asset of institutional scale; no sale signals. Branded F&B: Charalambides Christis, the #1 dairy (assembled by Charalambides/Shiacolas from Vivartia Cyprus in 2011-14 in what was then the largest Cyprus food deal), is the credible €25m+ succession/secondary conversation; Zorbas Bakeries (61 stores plus a New York concept) is a credible growth-minority ticket - the family has publicly case-studied US expansion [REPORTED, HBS case]. Corrections that matter for origination lists: KEO plc is anchored by the Church of Cyprus (27.09%), not Photiades; Lanitis Bros has been Coca-Cola HBC's bottler since 2006. Everything else is strategic-owned or micro-cap.

**VERDICT: underserved - two names deep.** Charalambides Christis and Zorbas are the pipeline; that is the whole pipeline.

### 2.7 Industrials and building materials

**Regional structure.** Greek heavy industry monetises through international listings, not sponsor exits: Titan America NYSE IPO (~$384-393m gross, Feb 2025), Metlen to the LSE and FTSE 100 (~£5.8b, Aug 2025) [FILED]. Control blocks are family/foundation-held; PE's only entries are distressed (Frigoglass restructuring: €110m of notes equitised, bondholders to 85% [FILED, ATHEX]).

**CYPRUS INSIDE THIS SECTOR.** One asset of scale: Vassiliko Cement Works (CSE-listed, FY2024 revenue ~€140m [REPORTED], Church of Cyprus among anchor shareholders; a reported Holcim stake could not be verified - NO RELIABLE DATA, registry check required). Anchor-shareholder structure makes it a stake-build, not a control deal. The rest of the manufacturing base is sub-scale; Muskita Aluminium's reported sale to Aluminco could not be verified - NO RELIABLE DATA.

**VERDICT: structurally uninvestable** - one listed asset with immovable anchors, nothing else at ticket size.

### 2.8 Transport, logistics, infrastructure and shipping services

**Regional structure.** Greek infrastructure repriced from privatisation-discount to fully-priced auctions inside the window: Thessaloniki Port's 67% went for €231.9m in 2018; Attiki Odos cleared at €3.27b upfront (GEK Terna, 2024); AIA's €785m IPO was ~12x covered with AviAlliance/PSP taking control; Egnatia Odos at €1.35b (GEK Terna binding offer - note, not €1.5b) [FILED/REPORTED per deal]. Core-infra money crowded in; the PE angle left is TAIPED's residual pipeline and brownfield marinas/ports.

**CYPRUS INSIDE THIS SECTOR.** Concessions exist and two events define the forward calendar. Limassol port: DP World (multipurpose + cruise) and Eurogate (containers) concessions from 2016-17 are performing - contrary to circulating rumour, there is no evidence of a DP World exit; it is expanding [FILED, DP World]. Larnaca: the government **terminated the €1.2b Kition Ocean Holdings port-and-marina concession on 27 May 2024**; the Ports Authority took the redevelopment in-house; a future re-tender is the live institutional entry point [REPORTED, CBN; Cyprus Mail 2026]. Hermes Airports (Larnaca + Paphos, nine-shareholder BOT led by Bouygues 22% with Egis and local groups) runs to **2031** - the re-tender or extension is the single largest Cyprus infrastructure event of the coming decade [REPORTED; INFERRED on significance]. The Limassol shipping cluster (Columbia, Bernhard Schulte, Interorient, Marlow) contributes €1.9b to the economy, >7% of GDP, with the registry at a 25-year high [REPORTED, Cyprus Mail 2026] - economically large, but German/Cypriot family-held service businesses with no capital need; the only wedge is services-adjacent (crewing tech, maritime fintech) [INFERRED].

**VERDICT: structurally uninvestable today; two dated options** - the Larnaca re-tender and Hermes 2031. Both are calendar entries, not pipeline.

### 2.9 Real estate and development (PE-relevant only)

**Regional structure.** Greece: listed REIC formation (Trade Estates, Noval, Dimand IPOs; BriQ-ICI merger to >€280m GAV) and the Ellinikon megaproject (Lamda: ~€1.1b capex by end-2025, €2.2b secured revenues) [FILED/REPORTED]. Foreign capital present: Hines, Invel/Prodea, Brook Lane.

**CYPRUS INSIDE THIS SECTOR.** The citizenship-by-investment era ended in November 2020; part of the Limassol tower pipeline resolved by non-construction, and replacement demand is Israeli (~4,000 Israeli-linked purchases across southern Cyprus since 2021 [REPORTED, Cyprus Mail]). Developers (Cybarco/Lanitis, Aristo, Pafilia, Leptos) are family-held with no institutional capital events - NO RELIABLE DATA on any PE entry. Institutional RE equity on the island is essentially absent outside hotel RE (HIP) and the MHV/Invel-legacy Landmark Nicosia. **The actual institutional RE market is the distressed/REO channel:** Gordian (Helix servicing), Themis, doValue Cyprus/Kedipes, with banks and credit acquirers listing ~€2.5b of property for sale [REPORTED, Ask-Wire]. The Church of Cyprus is the island's largest landholder (property estimated >€1.5b, stakes in KEO, Vassiliko, Hellenic Mining) - a land-JV counterparty, not a seller [REPORTED/INFERRED].

**VERDICT: structurally uninvestable in development equity; the REO/servicing channel is the institutional market** and is already owned by Apollo- and PIMCO-linked platforms.

## STEP 3 - WHAT STRATEGY ACTUALLY WORKS HERE

| Sector | Strategy that worked in the region | Why that one | Where the Western import fails / local specificity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Banks / FS | Distressed-to-book equity ahead of sovereign re-rating; NPL/servicing platform ownership | The state was a forced seller (HFSF) and banks were forced deleveragers (Hercules, Helix); buyers were paid for balance-sheet risk that then normalised | Classic FIG buyouts never happened - regulators and politics preclude sponsor control of banks. Cyprus variant: the winning buyers were Greek strategics; funds only ever got the paper, not the franchise |
| Healthcare | Buy-and-build to national-champion scale, exit to sovereign strategic (CVC/HHG playbook) | Fragmented doctor-owned supply + reimbursement systems (EOPYY, GESY) that convert private capacity into contracted volume | Imported roll-up math works only with a reimbursement backbone; in Cyprus GESY created it in 2019 - which is exactly when consolidation started. No Western analogue needed |
| TMT | Take-private of listed software at regulatory-tailwind moments (e-invoicing); founder-partnership minorities | ATHEX illiquidity made control cheap relative to recurring revenue; founders needed capital partners, not exits | Growth-equity-at-scale fails: the region's largest tech profit pools (Cyprus CIF/gaming) do not sell equity at any price; InstaShop's >90% write-down shows imported q-commerce math failing |
| Energy | Platform sales to sovereign strategics; cornerstone stakes in state champions (CVC/PPC) | The buyers underwriting 2030 targets are Gulf sovereigns with cost of capital no fund matches | Western-style RES platform building priced out at €1.7-2.7m/MW; the Cyprus-specific trade (BESS against 28% curtailment) has no Western analogue because no Western grid curtails at that rate uninterconnected |
| Hospitality | Asset-backed OpCo/PropCo purchase from family owners who retain management, plus capex repositioning (HIP model) | Families sell real estate before they sell identity; management retention converts a succession blocker into a deal feature | The full-platform luxury build (Sani/Ikos) needs Greek scale; in Cyprus only the HIP model has closed. This is the strategy with a proven Cyprus print |
| Consumer / F&B | Brand-by-brand dismemberment (Vivartia) and listed-platform roll-up (Ideal) | Exit depth at €100-250m EV is in trade buyers, not IPOs; conglomerates traded below sum-of-parts | Cyprus variant is succession capital into two named families - buy-and-build has almost nothing to bolt on in a sub-1m-consumer market |
| Infrastructure | Concession auctions and privatisation IPOs | State calendar creates the flow | Cyprus flow is state-failure-generated (Larnaca termination, Vasilikos arbitration) - the local strategy is re-tender readiness, not auction participation |
| Real estate | Distressed/REO channel via servicers | The paper changed hands at 40-50% of GBV in 2019-2023; the property is monetised now | Development equity has no institutional market in Cyprus; the REO trade is already owned by the credit funds that bought the NPLs |

**The general pattern [INFERRED]:** in Greece, the working strategies are exit-driven - build or buy the national champion, sell to sovereign capital. In Cyprus, no strategy that begins with an auction works, because there are no auctions; every executed institutional entry was either creditor-led (Remedica, the NPL complex, Attica-style swaps next door) or bilateral-family (Olympic Lagoon). A Cyprus coverage effort that waits for processes will book zero deals; one that runs a named-family origination list (Louis perimeter, Charalambides Christis, Zorbas, Medochemie, Kanika-model hotel RE) is running the only strategy with executed precedent.

## STEP 4 - WHAT THE MAP REVEALS

Three findings, ranked by how much they should move an IC. None appears in a Big 4 regional report.

**FINDING 1 - The "undiscovered market" window in Cyprus closed before any fund arrived, and it was strategics who closed it.** The intuitive IC thesis - small market, no competition, first-mover returns - is roughly five years too late. Between 2022 and 2025, every institutional-grade Cypriot asset that could be bought was bought: the #2 and #4 banks by Greek banks (~€1.4b+ combined), the insurance leader by a bank, the premium hospital capacity by a CVC platform en route to Abu Dhabi, the generics manufacturer by its lenders, and the first hotel by Blackstone's platform. Not one of these was a contested sponsor process; not one produced a filed multiple. What remains is not an empty market but a residue: family successions, one cash shell (Demetra), one sponsor-owned exit-in-waiting (Remedica), and two concession dates. **Falsified by:** evidence of a primary, non-creditor-led sponsor buyout of a Cypriot operating company above €50m EV that this research missed, or two or more such deals occurring in the next 24 months at entry multiples below the Argos band - which would show contested supply still exists at a discount.

**FINDING 2 - Cyprus has a leverage vacuum that no amount of bank liquidity will fill, which mechanically selects the strategies that can work.** The paradox is precise: €17.4b of excess liquidity, deposits over loans by €30.8b, LCR at 319% - and zero sponsor-finance capacity, with no Cypriot bank running a disclosed leveraged-finance business and no local private credit fund in existence [FILED liquidity data; NO RELIABLE DATA to the contrary on sponsor lending]. This is not cyclical caution; it is the post-2013 supervisory settlement of a two-bank system whose collateral culture is real estate. The consequence for underwriting: any Cyprus deal is equity-heavy or imports debt (Greek banks, international credit), so returns must come from entry price, asset backing or operational growth - never from leverage. That is why the only executed models are asset-backed (hotel RE) and creditor-led (debt-for-equity). A mid-market buyout fund's standard model does not price here. **Falsified by:** a Cypriot bank underwriting a sponsor acquisition facility on market terms, or a private credit fund opening a Cyprus lending mandate - either would be visible in a single transaction announcement and would materially improve the market's accessibility.

**FINDING 3 - The island's largest genuinely growing profit pool is structurally un-buyable, so Cyprus's PE-relevant deal flow is generated by the state and by creditors, not by companies.** ICT is the fastest-growing sector (GVA €3.59b, +11.3% real in 2024; services surplus 7.5% of GDP [FILED via CyStat/CBC]) - and it is owned by founders and emigres (Exness, Wargaming, TheSoul, BrainRocket, XM) who are unlevered, un-successioned, and carry provenance-diligence burdens most LP bases will not accept. Meanwhile every institutional transaction actually available over the next five years traces to a state or creditor decision: the Larnaca port re-tender (state terminated the €1.2b concession in 2024), the Hermes Airports 2031 concession expiry, Kedipes/REO disposals, servicer exits (Themis, Gordian, doValue Cyprus), the Remedica secondary, and whatever GSI's fate does to the RES/BESS pipeline. Coverage should therefore be calendar-driven and counterparty-driven (ministries, AMCs, sponsors' portfolio desks), not sector-screening-driven - the opposite of how a Western coverage map is built. **Falsified by:** a first control or structured-minority sale by any of the named founder-held ICT/CIF companies to an institutional buyer - one such print would reopen the sector and invalidate the "un-buyable" premise; equally, failure of both concession events to reach market by 2032 would show even the state-generated flow is illusory.
