## 1. BEAR CASE

*Argued in the voice of the acquirer's commercial due-diligence lead. Steelmanned; the position taken in Section 2 must survive this.*

You are being asked to underwrite a 39-year-old family tyre importer on which not one financial figure exists in the public domain. Not revenue, not gross margin, not headcount. The entire "premium distributor" narrative rests on the target's own homepage. Here is what the evidence we do have actually says:

1. **The premium claim is false on its own brand list.** Lagrome's marketing says it "represents premium tires" [SOURCED - lagrome.com.cy homepage snippet, 2026-08-29]. Its published brand portfolio is Yokohama plus Apollo, Vredestein, ATG (Alliance/Galaxy), JK Tyre, Nexen, BKT, Deestone, Doublestar, Otani, Sunfull, Fortune, Aeolus, Torque [SOURCED - lagrome.com.cy brand pages, snippets, 2026-08-29]. Partner-country customs mirrors show its supply base is India and Vietnam - ATC Tires (ATG), CEAT, JK Tyre, COFO Vietnam [SOURCED - 52wmb buyer profile 26003570; Trademo profile 40603908; Volza profile 15208801, all 2026-08-29]. Every genuinely premium agency in Cyprus is held by somebody else: Michelin by CTC Automotive, Bridgestone by Lefkaritis Bros, Continental by MPM Imports (claimed), Goodyear/Dunlop by D2D, Pirelli by Karagiorgis (Section D). This is a budget-Asian import house with one upper-mid flag brand. If management misdescribes its own portfolio in public, treat every other management representation as unverified.

2. **The one asset that matters may not survive the transaction.** The only economically distinctive asset visible from outside is the claimed exclusive Yokohama agency [SOURCED - lagrome.com.cy and Omonoia FC sponsorship announcement, Oct 2018, snippets]. Distribution agreements in this industry are standardly terminable on change of control, and manufacturers demonstrably reclaim distribution when circumstances change: Bridgestone lifted 42 REIFF branches out of the Fintyre insolvency in 2020 [SOURCED - Tyrepress, Apr-May 2020]; Continental is exiting its own BestDrive France network [SOURCED - Tyre-Trends, 2024-25]; Pirelli has since 2024 designated its Greek subsidiary as the export hub for the Balkans **and Cyprus** [SOURCED - mononews.gr on Elastika Pirelli FY2024], showing exactly how a manufacturer re-routes an island market through a regional hub when it wants to. No Lagrome agency contract has been seen. Until the change-of-control and assignment clauses are read, the fair assumption is that the agency book does not transfer.

3. **The market is small, flat-to-structurally-unhelpful, and already spoken for.** Total Cyprus imports are on the order of 700,000-800,000 tyres a year [SOURCED - Cyprus Audit Office estimate reported by Cyprus Mail, 4 Feb 2022]. At retail that is a market plausibly in the tens of millions of euros (Section C) - a market the size of a single provincial European dealer group, on an island, with at least ten established importer-distributors and a new Point S franchise network (master franchise: Eltenor; 11 franchisees signed by Feb 2026) rolling up the independent fitter channel [SOURCED - Tyrepress/Tire Review, Feb 2026]. The structural trend - budget-Asian substitution - is the one segment where Lagrome already sits, which means its niche is precisely the one with the lowest barriers to entry: anyone with a letter of credit can import Sailun or Linglong, and two competitors (Kapodistrias with Sailun, Katsambas with Linglong) already do at scale.

4. **The economics of the model are proven thin at 100x the scale.** Goodyear's own Greek distribution subsidiary - a manufacturer-owned operation at EUR 56.4m revenue - earns an 18.8% gross margin and a 1.4% net margin [SOURCED - financialreport.gr/newmoney.gr, FY2025 accounts]. Fintyre went from EUR 400m to EUR 1b of revenue and into insolvency inside three years [SOURCED - Bloomberg Law, Tyrepress, 2020]. American Tire Distributors has been through Chapter 11 twice [SOURCED - Modern Tire Dealer, 2018 and 2024]. Tyre distribution is a working-capital treadmill: roughly a quarter of annual sales sits in inventory (Inter Cars: 25.8% [DERIVED - PLN 5,028m inventory / PLN 19,473m revenue, FY2024 preliminary results]). We cannot see Lagrome's inventory, its ageing, its receivables, or its shareholder loans. In a business like this, that is not a gap in the data room - that IS the business.

5. **Governance and disclosure signals point the wrong way.** The registered office differs from the operating HQ [SOURCED - registry aggregators vs company site]. The social-media presence is run as "Yokohama Cyprus", not Lagrome - the brand equity being built belongs to a manufacturer that can leave. Directors and secretary are three individuals of the same family (Savva) [SOURCED - registry aggregators]; succession and key-man risk are self-evident. A civil appeal against the company ran from at least 2016 to a Supreme Court decision in June 2025 [SOURCED - CyLaw 2025 index], subject and quantum unknown. ISO 9001 is claimed but appears in no certification-body register we could find. And there is no evidence of e-commerce checkout, fleet contracts, or any capability that would distinguish the firm from the eleven other importers on the island.

**Bear-case conclusion:** this is a sub-scale, family-run, budget-tier import house in a micro-market, whose only differentiating asset is an agency that may be lost by the act of buying the company, whose economics are invisible, and whose segment has the lowest entry barriers in the industry. The only price at which it is safely buyable is one anchored to tangible net working capital, with the agency book paid for only if, and after, it transfers.

## 2. POSITION

**The position, in plain terms:** Lagrome is not a strategic asset; it is a working-capital annuity with an agency lottery ticket attached. The business almost certainly makes money - a four-branch importer does not survive 39 years, sponsor a first-division football club, and hold an exclusive national agency for a top-ten global tyre brand while losing money - but nothing about it commands a franchise premium. It is buyable, and potentially attractively buyable, for exactly one reason: nobody can price it, because nobody has looked. The accounts are retrievable from the Registrar for tens of euros, the seller is a family with visible succession exposure, and the plausible buyer universe for a Limassol tyre wholesaler is close to zero institutional competition. Mispriced small assets in unfashionable niches are where disciplined buyers make money - provided the price assumes the bear case and the structure makes the seller carry the agency risk.

**Defence against the bear case, point by point:**

- *Premium claim (bear point 1):* conceded on the facts - and it cuts both ways. The correct read is not "management lies" but "management markets". The budget-Asian tier is where the Cyprus market is going, not where it is leaving: Goodyear's own Greek management report describes inflation-driven migration from premium to value tiers benefiting Asian brands [SOURCED - newmoney.gr/financialreport.gr, FY2025]; PC/LT tyre imports into Europe from China/ASEAN/India rose 12% y/y in early 2025 [SOURCED - ETRMA Q1 2025 update]. Lagrome is positioned with, not against, the structural current. The peer set is rebuilt accordingly in Section E; benchmarked against the correct comparators (MPM, Katsambas, Kapodistrias-Sailun), a defensible mid-market position emerges. What is NOT conceded to the upside: none of this supports paying a premium multiple.
- *Agency transfer (bear point 2):* fully conceded as the first-order deal question. The answer is structural, not analytical: no binding offer before the Yokohama (and Apollo/ATG/JK) agreements are read; purchase price split so that agency-dependent value is paid via earn-out or deferred consideration conditional on written manufacturer consent (Section H). If the seller refuses that structure, that is itself the diligence finding.
- *Small flat market (bear point 3):* conceded on size, contested on implication. A 700-800k unit island market with no winter-tyre season, the EU's 4th-highest car density, a fleet where 66% of cars are over ten years old, and 60-64% of parc additions arriving as used imports [all SOURCED - Section C] is a stable, replacement-driven, non-discretionary demand pool ideally suited to budget tyres - small, but annuity-like. Nobody builds a fortune here; a well-bought distributor compounds quietly.
- *Thin economics (bear point 4):* conceded that gross margins are structurally ~15-20% at distributor level - but the comparison set that matters is small owner-operated importers, not manufacturer subsidiaries. The owner of a EUR 5-8m revenue import house with paid-down stock and family cost discipline can extract high-single-digit EBITDA margins that a corporate cannot (unverified for Lagrome - this is precisely what the HE32/financial-statement pull must establish before any offer). The valuation frame (Section H) prices off actual filed EBITDA at 3.5-5.5x, not off hope.
- *Governance opacity (bear point 5):* conceded, and priced. Every item on that list is resolvable for under EUR 1,000 of registry and court fees plus one week of counsel time (Section M workplan). Opacity that is cheap to resolve is an opportunity in a seller's-information-vacuum, not a reason to walk - walking comes only if the resolution produces one of the kill criteria (Section L).

**Bottom line:** proceed to paid diligence now (cost: low hundreds of euros, one to two weeks), negotiate exclusivity cheaply, and price any offer at 3.5-5.5x verified normalised EBITDA, cash-free/debt-free, with a 12-month-average NWC peg, aged-stock write-downs, and the agency book paid only on transfer. Do not proceed to a binding offer until the four kill-criteria checks in Section L clear.

## L. KILL CRITERIA

The three findings that would invalidate the acquisition recommendation outright:

1. **Agency non-transfer:** the Yokohama distribution agreement (or the agreements behind a majority of gross profit, once the brand-margin split is seen) is non-assignable and the manufacturer declines written consent, or signals hub-routing via Greece. The remaining business is a commodity importer with fitting bays - buy stock, not shares, or walk.
2. **The accounts refute viability:** the Registrar pull shows chronic non-filing, qualified audits, negative or de-minimis normalised EBITDA, or a balance sheet where shareholder loans fund aged stock (inventory days materially above ~120 with no ageing provision). Any one of these converts the thesis from "opaque but sound" to "opaque because unsound".
3. **Working capital does not survive contact:** physical stock-take and receivables ageing show recoverable NWC materially below book such that the NWC-pegged price nets to zero or negative - i.e. the annuity component, the only unconditional value in the deal, is not there. (Related but subordinate: the premises turn out to be shareholder-owned with an off-market lease and no willingness to grant a long lease at arm's length - survivable via price, but combined with 1 or 2 it kills.)
