# ICP: Cyprus redomiciliation and tax residency (Book 1, Vertu)

Researched 12 September 2026. Sources named inline. Figures marked **[verify]**
are not confirmed and must not be used in client-facing material until they are.

## The Cyprus product as it stands in 2026 - not as it stood in 2025

The reform that took effect 1 January 2026 changed the pitch materially. Anyone
still selling "12.5%" is selling last year's jurisdiction.

| Feature | Position from 1 Jan 2026 |
|---|---|
| Corporate income tax | **15%**, up from 12.5%, aligning with the OECD global minimum |
| Non-dom SDC exemption | 17 years, then extendable years 18-22 for a **€250k** lump sum, and years 23-27 for a further €250k |
| SDC on dividends (domiciled individuals) | Cut from 17% to **5%** |
| Deemed dividend distribution | **Abolished** |
| SDC on rental income | Abolished |
| Stamp duty on documents | Abolished |
| IP box effective rate | 2.5% [verify post-reform] |
| Inheritance tax / property wealth tax | None |
| 60-day residency rule | Available where no other state's residency is triggered and a Cyprus tie exists |
| 50% exemption on employment income | Above the qualifying threshold [verify current threshold post-reform] |

Source: Cyprus tax reform commentary, January 2026 (Sovereign Group, Kendris,
Philippou & Associates). Confirm each figure against the enacted law before use.

**The 15% rate is a selling problem and an opportunity.** It removes the
headline-rate argument against Ireland and puts Cyprus level with the global
floor. What survives is the personal side: non-dom, no inheritance tax, the 5%
SDC, and the treaty network. Lead with the personal position, not the CIT rate.

## Why they are actually leaving - the four source markets

The trigger is always in the departure jurisdiction, never in Cyprus. Cyprus is
the destination; the pain is at home. Outreach that leads with Cyprus sunshine
competes with every estate agent. Outreach that leads with the departure problem
does not.

### Sweden - the strongest and most underserved market

| Driver | Detail |
|---|---|
| Portugal is closed | Sweden unilaterally terminated the Portugal treaty effective 1 January 2022 over Portugal's NHR treatment of Swedish pensions. Swedish-source pensions are now taxed in Sweden at 25% SINK regardless of Portuguese treatment |
| NHR itself is gone | Portugal closed NHR to new entrants from 1 January 2024; the replacement (IFICI / "NHR 2.0") is narrower, aimed at research and innovation roles |
| Spain is unattractive | Wealth and solidarity taxes, Modelo 720 reporting [verify current position] |
| The real problem | **Väsentlig anknytning.** Skatteverket can treat a deregistered emigrant as still Swedish-resident. Where the person was resident 10+ years, a five-year presumption applies and **the burden of proof is on the taxpayer**. Factors: a home available in Sweden, spouse or children remaining, directorships, Swedish assets, club memberships |
| Plus the 10-year rule | Capital gains on Swedish shares remain taxable in Sweden for up to ten years post-departure unless a treaty overrides |
| Community | Roughly 2,000 Swedes resident in Cyprus, organised through the Swedish Association in Cyprus (founded 1991) - a warm channel that already exists |

The Swedish client is not looking for a Cyprus apartment. They are looking for
someone who can get them out of Sweden cleanly after the Portugal route failed.
That is a documentation and evidence exercise in Sweden, coordinated with a
Cyprus landing. Estate agents cannot do it. Most Cyprus accountants cannot
either.

### United Kingdom - deadline-driven, largest absolute volume

| Driver | Detail |
|---|---|
| Non-dom regime abolished | 6 April 2025, after two centuries. Long-resident non-doms now face worldwide taxation as it arises |
| Replacement is narrow | The 4-year FIG regime requires 10 consecutive years of prior non-residence. HMRC's own figures suggest roughly 14,800 can use it against 73,700 who previously claimed non-dom status |
| IHT now residence-based | With a long tail after departure |
| The live deadline | **Temporary Repatriation Facility**: pre-April-2025 offshore income at 12% in 2025-26 and 2026-27, rising to 15% from 2027-28. That is a dated, expiring arbitrage - the single best reason for a UK prospect to act this quarter rather than next year |
| Existing base | Roughly 30,000 British nationals already resident in Cyprus. Post-Brexit they are third-country nationals, so the immigration route matters |
| Caution | The "16,500 millionaires left the UK" figure is a contested Henley forecast, not settled fact. Do not put it in client material - it is the first thing a sceptical adviser will attack |

### Germany - highest fee per client, hardest problem

| Driver | Detail |
|---|---|
| The blocker | **§6 AStG Wegzugsteuer.** Applies where the person was German-resident 10 of the last 12 years and holds shares above roughly €500k in value, or at least 1% of a corporation. Departure is a deemed disposal at market value, taxed at the marginal rate on 60% of the gain under the partial income method |
| The Cyprus advantage | For moves within the EU/EEA the liability can be paid in **seven equal annual instalments, interest-free** - but the deferral is not automatic. It must be formally requested in the departure-year return via Anlage ZA |
| Why this is the wedge | A German founder with a GmbH cannot simply move. Getting the election wrong is a seven-figure error. Nobody selling apartments can advise on it |
| Also | Extended German inheritance-tax exposure after emigration [verify the tail period] |

### China and other third-country wealth

Motivation is residency and asset diversification rather than tax arbitrage, and
the qualifying route is investment-linked. Treat as a separate, lower-priority
segment: longer cycles, heavier KYC, and source-of-funds work that must be done
properly before any engagement.

## The bear case on the stated differentiation

K's framing is "we are authorised and credible versus any real estate agent who
wants to sell them an apartment." Half of that is right and half is a trap.

It is right against DOM and bbf - but they are not the competition for the fee.
They give the residency work away to close a property sale, which means they can
outspend any advisory firm on marketing while charging the client nothing for
the advice. Competing with a subsidised product on the same ground loses.

It is wrong against CYAUSE. Tramountanellis is an FCA, ICPAC-regulated, has been
publishing this exact pitch for years, has the search rankings, and has built a
lead-generation platform on top. "We are authorised" does not differentiate
against him - it matches him, late.

So the defensible position is not "authorised Cyprus adviser." It is **the firm
that solves the departure problem, not the arrival problem**: väsentlig
anknytning evidence for Swedes, §6 AStG deferral for Germans, TRF timing for
UK non-doms. Every competitor above sells the Cyprus end. The client's actual
risk, and the fee that follows it, sits at the other end.

That repositioning also changes the channel, which is the commercially important
consequence. See below.

## The two ICPs

### ICP-A: Corporate - groups needing a Cyprus SPV or holding structure

| Dimension | Filter |
|---|---|
| Jurisdiction of origin | UK, Germany, Sweden, Netherlands, Israel, UAE-linked EU groups |
| Sector | Sector-agnostic. Priority: technology and IP-heavy (IP box), shipping and logistics (tonnage tax), funds and SPV structures, e-commerce holding groups |
| Size | Revenue €2m-€100m, or any size where the Cyprus entity is a holding, IP or licensing vehicle |
| Trigger | Group restructuring announced; IP migration; a new EU market entry; substance challenge in the current jurisdiction; a Pillar Two threshold question; existing provider lost |
| Buying unit | Beneficial owner, group CFO, or the incumbent adviser |
| Disqualifiers | Shells with no filed activity; anything failing sanctions or adverse-media screening; existing Vertu clients; entities whose current provider is a referral partner; targets whose only interest is a nameplate with no substance - they become the compliance problem later |

### ICP-B: Private clients - HNWI tax residency

Segment by departure jurisdiction, because the trigger differs in each.

| Segment | Profile | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| **Swedish founder or principal, post-exit** | Sold or is selling a business, 10-year rule exposure, needs väsentlig anknytning severed properly. Often already tried or considered Portugal | **1st.** Highest need, weakest incumbent coverage outside Sweden, and a live reason the old route failed |
| **German GmbH shareholder** | 1%+ or €500k+ holding, §6 AStG exposure, needs the EU instalment election | **1st.** Highest fee per engagement, hardest problem, least competition |
| **UK former non-dom** | Long-resident, ineligible for FIG, has a TRF window closing | **2nd.** Largest volume, most competitive, but the TRF deadline gives urgency |
| **Swedish or Nordic pensioner** | Post-treaty-termination, 25% SINK on pension | 3rd. Real volume, lower fee, price-sensitive |
| **Digital nomads** | Remote-employed, DNV route | **4th - deprioritise.** The quota doubled to 1,000 permits a year in October 2025, which caps the whole segment; the DNV gives no direct permanent-residency path; and this client buys a permit, not a structure |
| **Chinese and other third-country** | Residency and diversification motive | 4th. Long cycles, heavy source-of-funds work |

Disqualifiers across ICP-B: anyone whose sole objective is concealment rather
than lawful relocation; anyone unwilling to give up the departure-state ties the
plan requires, since the plan then fails and the failure is attributed to Vertu;
PEPs and sanctions exposure; and anyone below the fee floor for whom a compliant
engagement cannot be run economically.

## Channel: why the adviser route beats direct-to-HNWI

Direct outreach to individual HNWIs is the obvious plan and the weaker one.

| | Direct to HNWI | Via departure-jurisdiction advisers |
|---|---|---|
| Universe | Hundreds of thousands, mostly unidentifiable as qualifying | A few hundred named firms in Sweden, Germany and the UK |
| Data cost | High. Individual contact data, poor qualification | Low. Firms are public, named, and reachable |
| Legal basis | **Personal data, individual subscriber.** Cold email to a private individual is the hardest ePrivacy case | **Corporate B2B.** Materially safer under GDPR Art.6(1)(f) and ePrivacy |
| Competition | Against CYAUSE's SEO and bbf's subsidised marketing | Against nobody - these firms need a Cyprus counterparty |
| Conversion | Cold, one client at a time | One relationship yields repeat referrals |
| Credibility | "Authorised" is a claim | Peer-to-peer between regulated professionals |

The adviser channel is where a €100 data budget goes furthest and where Vertu's
ICPAC and ACA standing is actually worth something. Run it first. Keep direct
HNWI outreach as a second wave, and when it runs, run it on the departure
problem as the hook - never on Cyprus lifestyle.

Adviser targets to build: Swedish tax advisory boutiques (the Skeppsbron Skatt
and Nomadtax tier), German Steuerberater and M&A boutiques advising GmbH
founders on exits, UK private client solicitors and IFAs with displaced non-dom
books, and Nordic wealth managers with relocating clients.
