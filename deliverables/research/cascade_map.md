**Cannabis / pharma column:** for almost every firm the honest answer is "unknown". Two structural points apply across the board [K]: (i) EU-GMP medical cannabis is legal in Greece under L.4523/2018 and most institutional ESG exclusion lists (SFDR Art. 8/9 funds, insurer-backed mandates) exclude *recreational* cannabis and/or "controversial" sectors, but rarely name medical cannabis explicitly; (ii) US-headquartered managers with US bank leverage providers or BDC vehicles are typically constrained by US federal illegality and bank-lender covenants even for non-US medical cannabis. Expect "no" by default from US-parented platforms and "case-by-case" from European independents.

## 7. What ABF investors actually finance

**Definition used by Cascade Debt and the market:** asset-based finance = private credit secured on pools of financial or hard assets whose cash-flows, not the borrower's EBITDA, service the debt. Five product types:

1. **Warehouse / senior facilities to originators** — a fund lends to a non-bank lender (consumer, SME, BNPL, auto, equipment) against an eligible pool, with advance rates and concentration limits. The borrower is the lender's SPV, not an operating company.
2. **Forward-flow agreements** — the fund commits to buy loans/receivables as originated at a pre-agreed price/yield.
3. **Portfolio acquisitions** — performing, re-performing or NPL pools bought outright (Arrow, Hoist, LCM, Balbec, Waterfall, Fortress/Bain in Greece).
4. **Specialty-finance equity / platform investments** — minority or control equity in the originator alongside the facility (Pollen Street, Quilam, VPC, Chenavari).
5. **Real-asset ABL** — loans against aircraft, ships, RE, equipment, inventory, royalties, litigation claims, infrastructure receivables (Castlelake, Marathon, NorthWall, Incus, Sienna, CarVal Clean Energy).

## 8. Do Thermo Lysi and Curity fit the ABF box?

**Thermo Lysi (IPL) — mostly no, with a narrow exception.** ABF underwrites *existing, granular, self-liquidating* collateral: pools of receivables, leased aircraft, seasoned mortgages, NPL portfolios with a servicer. A €118m single-asset, first-of-a-kind, pre-construction pyrolysis plant has none of that: collateral is future plant and equipment with uncertain liquidation value, cash-flow depends on a licensor (Pyrum) with sub-€15m revenue and a delayed breakeven [S: research_pyrolysis §5.5], and the only European tyre-pyrolysis precedents show grant + strategic equity + offtaker-linked debt (BASF, bp, Vitol/VTTI) rather than third-party ABF or infra-fund senior debt [S: research_pyrolysis §2.3].
