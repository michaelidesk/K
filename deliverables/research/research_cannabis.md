**Prepared for:** CPEF I Curity (sub-fund of Impactus RAIF VCIC Plc), Cyprus
**As of:** 22 September 2026
**Scope:** EU-GMP medical cannabis cultivation/processing; lenders, credit funds, banks, market fundamentals, distress evidence

> **Method and limitations (read first).** This briefing was compiled from ~30 web-search result digests (search budget of 200 calls/session was exhausted). Direct page retrieval (WebFetch) was blocked by the network egress proxy for every domain attempted (sec.gov, eib.org, ebrd.com, businessofcannabis.com, prohibitionpartners.com, cannabis-europa.com, mjbizdaily.com, regulator sites, law-firm sites, Wikipedia, etc.). Each fact below therefore carries the URL the search engine returned it from, but the underlying page was **not** opened and figures were not checked against primary documents. Treat every number as "reported" and re-verify from the cited primary source before use in an IC memo or lender discussion. Items that could not be confirmed, or where sources conflict, are collected in the final section.

## 0. Executive summary (credit view)

| Theme | Finding | Implication for CPEF I Curity |
|---|---|---|
| Dedicated cannabis credit in Europe | **No** specialist cannabis lender (Chicago Atlantic, AFC Gamma, Pelorus, IIP, NewLake, Silver Spike) has a documented European loan. All disclose US state-licensed operator focus. | European borrowers have historically relied on (i) strategic/corporate acquirers, (ii) equity + secured convertible loan notes from existing shareholders, (iii) small domestic bank lines (e.g. LGP/NAB A$2m at 7.68%), (iv) grants. A standalone senior secured lender for a Greek/Cypriot cultivator would be a first-of-kind ask. |
| Observed European debt instruments 2022-26 | Unsecured convertible note (Organigram→Sanity, €11.5m, 10% simple, 36 months, June 2024); secured convertible loan note (Avextra, €7.4m, May 2026, existing investors); acquirer-level senior secured facility (Organigram/ATB, $20m term drawn, April 2026); assumed local debt in Portuguese asset deals (RPK ~$5.1m, 2022). | Realistic pricing anchor for European cannabis convertibles: ~10% cash coupon + conversion optionality; US senior secured cannabis credit clears at 13-16% yields (AFCG 13.2%, REFI 15.8% at 30 June 2026). |
| German demand | Imports 201,094 kg in 2025 (vs 72,706 kg 2024, 32.5 t 2023); pharmacy sell-out >€1bn (€1.15bn). | Volume growth real, but Q4 2025 (56,625 kg) fell vs Q3 (59,076 kg): growth stalling. |
| German price | Avg patient price/g: €8.33 (Jan 2025) → €5.23 (Dec 2025) → €4.52 (Mar 2026); forecast to approach €4 by late 2026. Domestic max price to pharmacies ~€4.30/g; BfArM tender wholesale reference €2.30/g. | Price compression is the central underwriting risk; cost-of-production leadership is the only defensible position. |
| German regulation | (a) GKV flower reimbursement removed from 30 July 2026 (~65,000 patients); (b) MedCanG amendment banning telemedicine-only prescription and mail order: cabinet 8 Oct 2025, first Bundestag reading 18 Dec 2025, hearing 14 Jan 2026; final passage status **unconfirmed** as of this briefing. | Both measures target the self-pay telemedicine channel that drove 2025 volume. Bear case = demand contraction of unknown magnitude in H2 2026/2027. |
| Supply | Portugal exported 79,883 kg in 2025 and >66,000 kg in H1 2026; INFARMED cut cultivation authorisations 38→27 (2025→June 2026). Canada shipped 93,006 kg to Germany in 2025 (46%). | Oversupply plus regulator-driven shake-out in Portugal; Greek/Cypriot new entrants face a saturated German channel. |
| Distress evidence | Clever Leaves Portugal wind-down (assets sold €2.5m); Flowr CCAA (Oct 2022) and Holigen/RPK chain sold twice (last for $2.0m); Panaxia selling operations to settle debts; Cannacare (PT) licence removed Mar 2026; Sanity Group sold to Organigram (€107.3m upfront) after 10% convertible; AFC Gamma 35.7% of US book on non-accrual. | Ample bear-case data points; lenders will price for this. |
| Greece / Cyprus | Greece: Law 4139/2013 as amended by 4523/2018 and 4801/2021; Tikun Olam (Examilia) exporting since Q4 2024; Curity Pharma (Larissa) operational licence Mar 2025; April 2026 draft bill amending 4139/2013; May 2026 retail flower crackdown. Cyprus: 2017 law / 2019 amendment allows 3 cultivation licences over 15 years; in Aug 2025 the Director of Pharmaceutical Services said the provision "remained on paper". | Greece is live (small); Cyprus has no operating cultivation framework in practice. |
| Banks / DFIs | German Sparkassen and some Volksbanken still refuse cannabis accounts; Volksbank Donau-Mindel financed GOC Nexus. EIB list (approved 19 June 2025) and EBRD list do not name cannabis but exclude activities illegal under host-country law / international agreements. No public Greek or Cypriot bank policy located. | Expect bank debt to be relationship-driven and small; DFI money is not formally excluded for lawful medical cannabis, but no precedent found. |

### 6.8 EU-GMP supply/demand balance and price-compression risk (synthesis)

- Demand: Germany absorbed ~201 t of imports in 2025 plus domestic tender output; growth flattened in Q4 2025 and faces two 2026 headwinds (GKV flower de-listing from 30 Jul 2026; pending MedCanG telemedicine/mail-order ban).
- Supply: Portugal alone exported ~80 t in 2025 and 66 t in H1 2026 (annualising ~130 t); Canada shipped 93 t to Germany in 2025; Greece and others are adding capacity. Portugal's regulator is simultaneously removing marginal licensees (38→27 cultivation).
- Price: patient-level €/g fell 46% in 15 months (Jan 2025–Mar 2026). Domestic tender ceiling to pharmacies ~€4.30/g implies importer/producer net-backs well below that; the BfArM tender reference of €2.30/g is the floor benchmark for domestic supply. Cannabis Europa: "price compression erodes sub-scale operator margins" ([Cannabis Europa](https://cannabis-europa.com/from-price-compression-to-ma-the-commercial-reality-of-european-cannabis/)).
- Lender implication: any base case must be stress-tested at ≤€2/g farm-gate for flower and assume no GKV reimbursement volume.

## 8. Implications for a CPEF I Curity debt raise (analyst view)

1. **No off-the-shelf lender.** The only lenders with cannabis expertise (Chicago Atlantic, AFC, Pelorus) are US-only by mandate and are currently managing non-accruals; the only fund with a stated European allocation (Artemis Fund VII, ~75% Europe) is an equity/hybrid investor. The realistic structures are (a) secured convertible loan notes from strategic or existing shareholders (Avextra 2026; Organigram/Sanity 2024 at 10%), (b) acquirer-level facilities (Organigram/ATB), (c) small relationship bank lines secured on real estate and plant (LGP/NAB at 7.68%), (d) state grants/incentives (Portugal 2020 €2.7m to SOMAÍ; Greek Investment Incentives Law).
2. **Pricing anchor.** 10% cash coupon + equity kicker for a European convertible; 13-16% for US senior secured cannabis credit; European growth-debt houses (Kreos/Claret/Atempo) have no disclosed cannabis policy either way and would require VC-backed, revenue-generating borrowers.
3. **Collateral.** Every European distressed exit above cleared at a fraction of invested capital (Clever Leaves €2.5m; RPK US$2.0m after ~US$9m+ invested). Lenders will assign low recovery values to EU-GMP plant; land/buildings in Greece/Cyprus and offtake contracts with German importers (Cantourage, Cansativa, Curaleaf/Pedanios, Organigram/Sanity) will matter more.
4. **Underwriting case.** Base case must use ≤€2.30/g reference (BfArM tender) trending lower, exclude GKV volume, and haircut telemedicine-driven demand pending the MedCanG amendment outcome.
5. **DFI route.** Neither EIB nor EBRD names cannabis in its exclusion list; both exclude activities illegal under host-country law. A lawful Greek/Cypriot EU-GMP pharmaceutical operation is not formally excluded, but no precedent exists and reputational screening is likely. Worth a formal enquiry rather than an assumption either way.
